Cargo hold cleaning for bulk carriers prepares the hold for the next cargo or inspection, and the standard is set by what comes next rather than what was carried before. A hold that looks clean to the eye can still fail a grain inspection because of loose rust scale, odour or a damp bilge well.

Bulk carrier holds are large, awkward spaces with frames, brackets and hidden corners. Cleaning them well is a methodical job, not a pressure-washing exercise. The vessels that pass inspection first time are the ones that agree the standard, plan the sequence and treat the hidden areas as seriously as the visible ones.

This guide covers the main standards, the sequence that works and the failures that recur.

## The standard is set by the next cargo

The first question for any hold cleaning job is not "how dirty is it" but "what is it being prepared for". A hold for coal, iron ore, grain or food-grade cargo requires different levels of cleanliness and different inspection criteria.

Send the provider:

- The previous cargo and how long it was carried
- The next cargo or inspection standard
- Hold numbers and any known damage or coating issues
- Hatch cover and bilge condition
- The charterer or surveyor specification
- The vessel's location and available working window

When that information is clear, the cleaning method, chemical choice and inspection plan follow naturally. When it is vague, the vessel risks paying for a standard that is either too low or unnecessarily high.

## Grain-clean, food-grade and general clean

The industry uses a range of descriptions. They are not always used precisely, so confirm what is meant before work starts.

| Standard | Typical expectation | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| General clean | Free of loose cargo and debris | Residue in hidden areas |
| Grain-clean | No residue, loose scale or odour | Rust scale and damp bilges |
| Food-grade | Higher hygiene and odour control | Contamination and chemical residue |
| Survey-ready | Meets the surveyor's stated criteria | Unclear or changing criteria |

Ask the surveyor or charterer to state the standard in writing where possible. A vague "clean and dry" instruction is open to interpretation, and interpretation is expensive when a vessel is waiting.

## The cleaning sequence that works

Hold cleaning follows a logical order. Skipping a step usually means repeating a later one.

1. Remove residual cargo by sweeping, shovelling or vacuuming.
2. Inspect the hold and record condition, damage and stubborn areas.
3. Wash with fresh or sea water, working from the top down.
4. Treat stains, odour and adhering residue with suitable chemicals.
5. Remove loose rust scale from frames, brackets and bulkheads.
6. Clean bilge wells, strainers, lines and non-return arrangements.
7. Rinse thoroughly and remove standing water.
8. Dry and ventilate the hold.
9. Inspect and photograph before presenting to the surveyor.

The order matters because washing before removing loose cargo spreads residue into corners, and drying before cleaning the bilge wells leaves a source of dampness.

## Bilge wells, frames and hidden areas

Surveyors and cargo inspectors know where residue hides. The common failure points are predictable:

- Bilge wells and bilge covers
- Strainer boxes and suction lines
- Under-frame and bracket areas
- Hatch coaming and drain channels
- Hatch cover seals and compression bars
- Ladders, handrails and recesses
- The area behind hold guards and fixtures

A cleaning team that inspects these areas before the surveyor does saves the vessel a second visit. The cost of clearing a bilge well properly is trivial compared with the cost of a failed inspection.

## Chemical use and residue control

Chemicals can help with stains, odour and heavy residue, but they introduce their own risk. Residue left on a hold surface can contaminate the next cargo just as surely as the original dirt.

Before chemical cleaning, confirm:

- Compatibility with the hold coating
- The correct dilution and contact time
- Whether fresh water rinsing is required
- How washings will be collected and disposed of
- Whether the chemical leaves an odour or film

Washings and slops must go to an approved reception route with documentation. A cleaning job that leaves the slop disposal to chance is not complete.

## Inspection and surveyor expectations

The final inspection is the moment the work is judged. Prepare for it rather than react to it.

- Clean the hold in the order the surveyor will inspect it.
- Have lighting available for dark corners.
- Keep a responsible officer available for questions.
- Photograph each hold at the agreed stage.
- Record any pre-existing damage before cleaning begins.

If a surveyor raises an item, fix it immediately where possible and document the fix. Arguing about an interpretation while the vessel waits rarely helps.

## Mistakes and limits

Common mistakes include starting without a defined standard, ignoring bilge wells, washing with dirty water, leaving chemical residue, and presenting the hold before it is dry.

There are limits that no provider can remove. Some stains are permanent, some coatings are damaged, and heavy scale may need surface preparation beyond cleaning. A credible provider identifies those limits early so the vessel can decide whether to accept, repair or renegotiate.

## Planning the window in a UAE port

In Dubai and the wider UAE, hold cleaning is usually squeezed between cargo operations. The working window, berth access and terminal permissions shape what can be done alongside. Some jobs are better completed at anchorage or before the berth window opens.

A short pre-cleaning meeting with the terminal, the surveyor and the cleaning supervisor is worth the time. It confirms the standard, the water and chemical supply, the waste route and the areas that will be inspected first. It also gives the vessel a single record of what was agreed, which is valuable if the next cargo or inspector raises a question later.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does grain-clean mean for a bulk carrier hold?

Grain-clean generally means the hold is free of residue, loose rust, odour and contamination that could affect a grain cargo or fail an inspector. The exact standard should be confirmed with the charterer or surveyor.

### How long does cargo hold cleaning take?

A straightforward hold may take a day or two, while heavy residue, staining or a demanding standard can take longer. Access, previous cargo and inspection requirements drive the schedule.

### Are bilge wells part of hold cleaning?

Yes. Bilge wells, strainers and lines are common failure points and should be cleaned, checked and tested as part of the scope.

### Can chemicals be used in cargo holds?

They can be used where the cargo history and coating allow, but residues must be controlled and rinsed properly. Confirm compatibility and disposal arrangements before use.
