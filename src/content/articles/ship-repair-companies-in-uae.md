The UAE repair market is large enough that almost any vessel requirement can attract a response. That is good news for competition and less good news for comparison. A shipyard, a technical service company and a coordinator with a list of subcontractors can all describe themselves as ship repair providers, but they do not carry the same risk, cost structure or accountability.

This guide sets out a practical way to compare providers across the UAE, with the questions that reveal the difference between a capable partner and a polished quotation.

## Three provider models

**Shipyards** are the right choice for drydocking, major structural work, underwater inspection and coating programmes. They have the facilities, but access and scheduling are driven by berth availability.

**Technical service companies** focus on engine, mechanical, electrical, fabrication, tank cleaning and automation work. They can mobilise to a vessel alongside or at anchorage and are often faster for targeted repairs.

**Coordination companies** manage a scope through a network of subcontractors. This can work well when the scope is broad, but you should know who is accountable for quality, safety and scheduling.

The best model depends on the job. A main engine overhaul may suit a technical specialist. A major hull programme may need a yard. A combined port-call requirement may need a coordinator who can bring several trades together.

## The comparison framework

### Scope and technical depth

Ask for the actual work steps, the trades involved and the supervision structure. A long service list is not evidence of capability. Ask who will be on board, what maker experience they have and how the work will be tested.

### Port coverage and mobilisation

A provider with a strong workshop but no access to your berth is not useful. Confirm coverage at Jebel Ali, Dubai Maritime City, Port Rashid, Khorfakkan, Fujairah or wherever the vessel will be. Ask who handles permits and gate access.

### Spare parts and logistics

Many repair delays are parts delays. Ask how the provider identifies parts, whether they can source genuine maker components, what the lead time is and how they handle alternatives. For Caterpillar, Yanmar and other major makers, serial and part numbers should be confirmed early.

### Compliance and safety

Check how the provider handles permits, gas testing, confined space entry, hot work, waste and class or flag requirements. A repair that creates a compliance problem is not a successful repair.

### Communication

Ask for a single coordinator, a reporting rhythm and an escalation contact. For work longer than a shift, daily photos and a parts status are minimum expectations.

### Commercial transparency

Compare the full scope, not the headline. Ask for assumptions, exclusions, additional-work rates and the treatment of standby time.

### Close-out

Confirm the completion report, test results, photographs, certificates and warranty terms before work starts.

## Comparison table

| Factor | Weak signal | Strong signal |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Vague service list | Written method and sequence |
| People | Unnamed subcontractors | Named supervisor and trades |
| Parts | "We can arrange" | Lead time and sourcing route |
| Access | General port coverage | Confirmed berth or anchorage access |
| Compliance | "We follow rules" | Permits, gas tests, waste route |
| Reporting | Ad hoc calls | Agreed daily update and escalation |
| Pricing | Single lump sum | Itemised scope, assumptions, exclusions |
| Close-out | Verbal handover | Report, photos, tests, warranty |

## A practical award checklist

Before you confirm a provider, make sure you can answer these questions:

- What exactly is included and excluded?
- Who will supervise the work?
- How will parts be sourced and verified?
- What permits and documentation are required?
- How will progress be reported?
- What happens if additional work is found?
- What evidence will the vessel receive at completion?
- What warranty or follow-up applies?

If a provider cannot answer these clearly, the quotation is not ready to compare.
